# Adversarial data-science review: "Global governance of embodied AI and robotics" (Stage 2)

Reviewer role: senior data scientist / applied statistician. Scope: `report/analysis_report.md`, `report/paper.md`, `report/supplement.md`, `tables/`, `figures/`, `data/master_table.csv`, `scripts/analysis_common.py`, `13_analysis_A.py`, `14_analysis_B.py`, `15_analysis_C.py`, `01_build_registry.py`, `07_assemble.py`, `09_reliability.py`. Every number below marked "recomputed" was reproduced from `data/master_table.csv` using the exact set definitions in `analysis_common.load()`; recomputation scripts are in the reviewer's scratchpad (`verify1.py` to `verify5.py`), not in the project.

## 1. Overall verdict

1. The pipeline is reproducible and the arithmetic is right: every headline number I recomputed (674/859/63/301/7; 43.0% vs 3.2%; OR 0.811, CI 0.778-0.845, n = 1,535; chi-square p = 0.0029, V = 0.19; median kappa 0.888/0.947; the cosine matrix; silhouette 0.183; Table 16) matches the text to the stated precision.
2. The inferences drawn from those numbers are, in several headline cases, not supported, because the embodied-AI set is 75.5% research outputs and the general-AI set is 5.8% research outputs: most "embodied vs general" differences are genre differences.
3. Stratifying the principle comparison by document class reverses or removes 7 of the 14 core-principle differences (e.g. accountability goes from "no difference" to embodied instruments being 19.6 points *lower*, Holm p = 0.008). Finding 4 and parts of Findings 6 and 7 need to be rewritten.
4. The "embodied governance instrument" denominator (n = 63) is 62% general-AI instruments that mention robots as examples, 35% rated low-relevance by the annotator itself, 32% landing or partial captures, and contains the EU AI Act twice (a third copy sits in the general set). The gap index and Finding 2/3/5/6 inherit all of this.
5. The "technical standards operationalise physical safety (100% / 83.3%)" claim rests on 12 documents of which 8 are ISO catalogue landing pages, two of them literally titled "ISO Store".
6. Four of the five Holm-significant 2025-26 topic shifts (Finding 8) disappear when document class and region are adjusted for; the trend-model odds ratios depend on the partial year 2026 and are fitted at 2.8 events per parameter.
7. Regional cosine distances of 0.01-0.04 are below the sampling-noise floor for the smaller regions (two random n = 26 halves of Europe are 0.047 apart), and within-region genre distance (0.25) exceeds every between-region distance.
8. Reliability is model-vs-same-vendor-model, not human-validated; the level-2 "substantive" codes and the mechanisms field (Jaccard 0.65) that drive Parts C-E are the least reliable fields.
9. Text and tables disagree in places (19 not 17 Holm-significant principles; 29 vs 39 binding general instruments; "rho 0.93 for principle rankings" is the correlation of *differences*; "6 pulled in by category labels" is actually 120).
10. Recommendation: major revision. The corpus and codebook are a genuine asset; the analyses need to be re-run with genre held constant, duplicates merged, non-substantive captures excluded from instrument-level claims, and intervals shown wherever n < 40.

## 2. Findings ranked by severity

### CRITICAL

**C1. Genre confounding drives the headline embodied-vs-general principle profile (Exec summary Finding 4; §5.1; Table 8; Figure 20; paper §4).**
- Problem: the embodied set is 509/674 = 75.5% research outputs; the general set is 50/859 = 5.8% research outputs and 45.6% press releases/web pages. Table 8 compares the two sets pooled, so the "embodied principle profile" is largely the "academic-paper principle profile".
- Evidence (recomputed, presence >= 1): within governance instruments only (63 vs 301) the differences change as follows: non-maleficence +12.3 pp pooled -> -0.8 (p = 1.0); freedom & autonomy +12.8 -> -6.2; dignity +11.6 -> -1.4; human oversight +7.4 -> +2.3; sustainability -14.5 -> +2.1 (and +0.2 within research); solidarity -12.4 -> -7.9 (+9.6 within research); inclusive growth -41.4 -> +6.9; robustness -10.8 -> +14.1; beneficence -8.8 -> +2.1. Responsibility & accountability is reported as "the one principle present at the same rate" (+3.1, p = 0.24), but within instruments it is -19.6 pp (Holm p = 0.008) and within research -15.4 pp: a Simpson's paradox. Under a Cochran-Mantel-Haenszel test stratified by doc_class, only 5 of the 14 core principles remain Holm-significant (transparency, fairness, accountability [in the *opposite* direction], privacy, inclusive growth) versus 13 of 14 pooled. The MH odds ratio reverses the pooled direction for accountability (0.63), sustainability (1.10) and robustness (1.39).
- Fix: replace Table 8/Figure 20 with a stratified or model-based comparison (logistic regression of each principle on set + doc_class + region, or CMH), and present research-only and instrument-only panels. Rewrite Finding 4 to say which differences survive within genre (essentially the six embodied-specific principles plus transparency/fairness/privacy/inclusive growth) and that embodied documents are *less* likely to invoke accountability within genre.

**C2. The "technical standards are the operational layer" claim rests on ISO catalogue landing pages (Exec summary Finding 5; §5.3; Table 9; Figure 23; Conclusion 2).**
- Problem: the "Technical standards (n = 12)" group is `doc_type == technical_standard` in the embodied set. Eight of the 12 have `page_quality == landing_or_index_page` (D0045 ISO/TC 299 page, D0129, D0162, D0218 "ISO - Robotics", D0397, D0398 "ISO Store", D1337, D1382 "ISO - Store"); three more are partial captures; only D0197 (Israeli SI 395, 1993) is a substantive capture. D0398 and D1382 have `binding_nature = not_applicable` and are store pages. D0218 ("ISO - Robotics", 693 words) was scored physical_safety = 2 and human-robot proximity = 2, i.e. the annotator inferred "substantive specification" from a catalogue title.
- Evidence: §9.4 and the supplement admit "three ISO landing pages"; the correct count is eight of twelve. "Physical safety ... substantively specified in 83.3%" = 10/12 of which at most 4 could have been read.
- Fix: drop landing pages from any instrument-level content claim (Table 9, Figure 23, Table S12/S13 "standards" columns), or re-capture the standards (abstracts/scope clauses are freely available). State n after exclusion; with n <= 4 report counts, not percentages.

**C3. The embodied-instrument denominator (n = 63) measures "general AI laws that enumerate robots", is inflated by duplicates, and includes low-relevance and non-substantive captures (Exec summary Findings 2, 3, 5, 6; §3.2; §4.4 Table 7 / Figure 18; Table 9; Table 10; Figure 6; Conclusions 1 and 3).**
- Evidence (recomputed): of the 63 embodied governance documents, 39 have `ai_scope = general_ai_with_embodied_provisions`, 22 were rated relevance = 1 ("low") by the annotator, 7 are landing pages and 13 partial captures. Of the "seven binding instruments", EO 14110 (D0915) and the AI Act copy D1060 are rated relevance = 1; Taiwan D0480 is a 204-word partial capture with confidence 0.40; D1060 and D1291 are the same Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 (the text acknowledges "captured twice" but still counts seven), and a third copy, D1010 "AI Act Regulation (EU) 2024/1689", is a binding instrument in the *general* set, so the same statute appears on both sides of the comparison. Among the 12 "proposals/drafts", the AI Act proposal appears three times (D1103 annexes, D1290, D1310) and the Israeli high-tech strategy twice (D0191, D1687). Instruments carry 2.7 category labels each versus 2.14 for research outputs, so category shares among instruments sum to 270% versus 214%; "autonomous road vehicles 46.0% of instruments" is 29 documents of which 5 are AI Act copies/drafts. Dropping only the five obvious duplicates moves AV to 44.8%, industrial manipulators 34.9 -> 32.8%, drones 22.2 -> 19.0%.
- Consequence: the governance-gap index (Table 7, Figure 18) is a difference between a research share and a "which robots do general AI laws list as examples" share, with Wilson CI for social robots 2/63 = (0.9%, 10.9%) and for AVs (34.3%, 58.2%). The qualitative conclusion (social robots have almost no instruments) is probably right; the magnitude (+39.9) and the ordering of the middle of the table are not estimable at this n.
- Fix: (i) merge duplicates before counting (see M6); (ii) define "embodied instrument" as relevance >= 2 *and* governance document, or report both definitions; (iii) report the gap as a ratio or log-odds with CI and a version using `primary_embodied_category` (one label per document) to remove enumeration inflation; (iv) say "six distinct binding instruments" and flag that two were rated low-relevance by the annotator.

**C4. The "turn from ethics to industrial policy and control" is a composition effect (Exec summary Finding 8; §7.2; Table 16; Figure 34; Conclusion 5; paper §4).**
- Problem: 2025-26 embodied documents differ from 2020-24 documents in genre and region, not only in date. Recomputed: research share 82.2% -> 69.3%, news 6.8% -> 12.4%, soft instruments 3.9% -> 6.4%; Europe 66% -> 40%, Asia 11% -> 27%.
- Evidence (recomputed): within research outputs only, industrial policy 8.7% -> 12.9% (p = 0.24), cybersecurity 6.1 -> 10.7 (p = 0.14), ethics principles 69.3 -> 62.9 (p = 0.22), HRI 64.3 -> 53.6 (p = 0.045, Holm 0.99). Logistic models adjusting for doc_class and region: industrial policy OR 1.33 (p = 0.31), ethics OR 0.77 (p = 0.20), HRI OR 0.93 (p = 0.73), cybersecurity OR 2.12 (p = 0.014, Holm 0.35). Only human oversight & control survives (research-only Holm p = 0.048; adjusted Holm p = 0.063). Four of the five asterisked changes in Figure 34 are explained by more Asian strategies and news in the newest layer.
- Fix: present the period comparison adjusted for doc_class and region (or within research outputs and within instruments separately); downgrade Finding 8 to "human oversight and control rises within genre; the other shifts reflect what was collected in 2025-26".

### MAJOR

**M1. Trend models are under-powered, near-separated, and driven by the partial year 2026 (§7.3; Table 17; Finding 8).**
- Evidence (recomputed): the models have 13 parameters; risk-based tiering has 33 events (EPV 2.8), conformity mechanism 48 (EPV 4.0); the largest fitted |coefficient| is 27 (quasi-complete separation on a region or class dummy). Excluding 2026: risk tiering OR 1.47 -> 1.26 (p = 0.13); conformity mechanism 1.22 -> 1.18 (p = 0.07); conformity principle 1.14 -> 1.11 (p = 0.09). Raw share of risk tiering by year: 3.6, 7.0, 4.8, 3.2, 5.8, 4.4% for 2020-25 and 13.8% in 2026, i.e. a single partial-year jump, not a trend.
- Fix: Firth or ridge-penalised logistic regression; year as period dummies; report with and without 2026; apply a 10-EPV rule or drop the mechanism models.

**M2. Regional cosine distances are reported without any notion of sampling error, and the regional structure is largely genre (§6; Figure 31; Table 14; Exec summary Finding 7; Conclusion 4).**
- Evidence (recomputed): two random halves of Europe's embodied set at n = 26 each (the International layer's n) are 0.047 apart on average (95th percentile 0.086); Oceania has n = 16. The reported Europe-USA 0.012, Europe-Oceania 0.029, Europe-Asia 0.044 and USA-Oceania 0.037 are therefore within noise. Bootstrap 95% intervals: Europe-Asia 0.029-0.079, Europe-Middle East 0.094-0.218, Europe-International 0.136-0.282. The cosine distance between Europe's *research outputs* and Europe's *governance documents* is 0.246, larger than any between-region distance; the International layer (0% research) is "furthest" because it is all instruments. The Middle East result is robust (0.18 from Europe within research outputs only).
- Fix: bootstrap the matrix, show the within-region split-half floor, and compute distances within genre (research-only; instrument-only where n permits). Drop Oceania (n = 16) from the dendrogram or mark it. Rewrite "Europe, the United States and Oceania form one cluster" as "are indistinguishable at this sample size".

**M3. "Regions differ in mechanism more than in principle" compares non-comparable tests (Table 15; §6; Exec summary Finding 7).**
- Problem: principle tests are on 640 embodied documents in 6 regions; mechanism tests are on 370 governance documents *of all kinds* (not embodied) in 8 regions including Africa (n = 17) and Oceania (n = 15). Cramér's V is upward-biased in small, sparse tables, so the ranking by V mixes effect with bias; several mechanism tables have expected counts < 5 (conformity: 3 cells; liability regime: 8; prohibitions: 5) and were tested by chi-square anyway (the code's Fisher fallback applies only to 2x2 tables).
- Evidence (recomputed): bias-corrected V is 0.000 for transparency and beneficence and 0.034 for non-maleficence; mechanism Vs shrink by about 0.03 (technical standards 0.337 -> 0.308). Embodied governance documents per region are 22, 12, 12, 6, 5, 4, 1, 1, so no mechanism divergence test "about embodied AI" is possible; Table 15 and Figure 25 describe AI governance in general.
- Fix: say so explicitly; use bias-corrected V and exact tests (Fisher-Freeman-Halton or permutation); either restrict to embodied instruments (and report counts) or move the mechanism-by-region material out of the embodied narrative.

**M4. Mechanism differences are tested on the wrong population and over-narrated (§5.4; Table 10; Figure 24; Exec summary Finding 6).**
- Evidence (recomputed): the Holm tests in Table 10 are on all documents, where 60 of the 75 embodied mentions of `liability_regime` are research papers. On governance instruments only (63 vs 301), the only Holm-significant differences are conformity assessment (36.5 vs 7.3%, p < 0.001), technical-standards reference (60.3 vs 30.6%, p < 0.001) and testing/sandboxes (47.6 vs 24.6%, p = 0.006). Liability regime (9.5 vs 3.0%, Holm p = 0.60), insurance (2 vs 0 documents, Holm 0.44), incident reporting (0.68), licensing (0.84), prohibitions (1.0), funding (0.84), education (1.0) and international cooperation (0.78) are not significant, yet the text lists all eleven as mechanisms embodied instruments use "more often" and the executive summary calls liability and insurance "embodied-specific".
- Fix: run and report the instrument-only tests (they are the ones the narrative uses); restrict Finding 6 to the three robust differences.

**M5. The embodied set is not what the text says it is (§1; Exec summary; §9.4; paper §3).**
- Evidence (recomputed): "documents ... with at least moderate relevance" and "6 were pulled in by category labels alone" are both wrong: 120 of 674 (17.8%) have relevance = 1 (65 research outputs, 24 news items, 18 soft instruments, 4 drafts, 2 binding instruments); the "6" is the number with `ai_scope = general_ai_only`. Four documents with `embodiment_status = none` are in the set. The 120 `general_ai_with_embodied_provisions` documents are split 93/27 between the embodied and general sets purely on whether a category label was emitted.
- Fix: correct the definition text; add a sensitivity row to Table 19 using relevance >= 2 only; consider treating `general_ai_with_embodied_provisions` as a third group rather than splitting it.

**M6. Deduplication failed on far more than the AI Act (§2 step 1; all instrument counts; Figure 6; Table 2; Table 13).**
- Evidence (recomputed): 29 groups of usable documents share an identical normalised `title_en` (41 excess documents); 27 of the 70 involved are governance documents: Ethics Guidelines for Trustworthy AI x5 (D1066, D1067, D1068, D1070, D1113), UNESCO Recommendation x2, Canada national AI strategy x2, Italian strategy x2, Continental AI Strategy x2, UK pro-innovation white paper x2, UAE stance x2, ISO/IEC TR 24028 x2, Civil Law Rules on Robotics x2 (D1299, D1304), plus the AI Act x3 and its proposal x3-4. Copies disagree on embodied-set membership (3 groups), doc_class (5) and year (4). Cause: `01_build_registry.py` applies the fuzzy-title rule (>= 0.93) only "within Europe V1<->V2 and across regions", so same-folder near-duplicates survive, and MinHash cannot link a landing page to the PDF it points at.
- Fix: add a within-region exact/fuzzy normalised-title pass (and a `title_en` pass after annotation), merge, and recompute 301/63/381, Figure 6 and Table 13; report the number of merged documents.

**M7. Lexicon validation is mis-described, and the actual agreement is weak (Exec summary Finding 10; §9.2; paper abstract: "Spearman rho 0.93 for principle rankings").**
- Evidence (recomputed from `15_analysis_C.py`): rho = 0.93 is the correlation across 20 principles of the *embodied-minus-general difference* under the two measures (`diff_ann` vs `diff_lex`), not of principle shares or rankings. Rank correlation of overall shares is 0.84; within the embodied set it is 0.51 (p = 0.02). Kappa between lexicon and annotation has median 0.41-0.47, with autonomy-level control 0.29, beneficence 0.27, military weapons 0.14, and the lexicon flags "military weapons" in 29.1% of documents against 4.8% annotated. This is a weak validation of excerpt-based annotation of long instruments (the AI Act copy D1291 is 97,174 words; the model saw ~14,000 characters).
- Fix: state what was correlated; report the within-set rank correlations; treat the lexicon check as a sanity check, not validation; for governance instruments consider chunked full-text annotation.

**M8. Reliability is model-vs-model from the same vendor, with no human gold standard, and is weakest exactly where the conclusions are (§9.1; Figure 35; Table S09; paper abstract "independent model").**
- Evidence (recomputed from `annotations/`): the "independent" annotator is Claude Opus under the same prompt as Claude Sonnet; shared training and prompt make agreement an upper bound on validity. Set membership is stable (kappa 0.91 inter-model, 0.95 retest). But the level-2 "substantive" distinction that underpins §5.2-5.3 and Finding 5 has binary inter-model kappa median 0.79 with dignity 0.60, non-maleficence 0.62, solidarity 0.49 (the report quotes quadratic-weighted 0-2 kappas of 0.77-0.98, which flatter the binary cut). Governance mechanisms: mean Jaccard 0.65; only 41.9% of documents receive identical mechanism sets from the two models; the mechanisms field underlies Tables 10, 11, 15, 17 and Figures 24-25. Stage-2 plan item 20 ("results re-run on the reliability subsample to show conclusions hold under the alternate annotator") was not delivered.
- Fix: human-code a stratified 100-150 document sample on set membership, doc_class, binding nature, the six embodied-specific principles at level 2 and mechanisms; re-run Tables 8-10 on the 160-document sample under the Opus annotations; label the second model "second LLM", not "independent".

**M9. Percentages on tiny cells without intervals (Table 9; Figure 23; Figure 25; Table 14; §5.4; §6; Exec summary Findings 5 and 7).**
- Evidence: binding instruments n = 7 (85.7% = 6/7; 42.9% = 3/7; 28.6% = 2/7; 14.3% = 1/7); Oceania governance documents n = 15 ("6.7%" = 1/15, "66.7%" = 10/15, "60.0%" = 9/15); Africa n = 17 ("94.1%" = 16/17, "88.2%" = 15/17); Canada embodied n = 9 ("89%"); International embodied n = 26; Middle East framing "60%" from n = 37. Wilson intervals are shown only in Figures 5 and 7. Wilson CI for 3/7 is (15.8%, 75.0%); for 1/15, (1.2%, 29.8%).
- Fix: report counts and Wilson intervals for every cell with n < 40; grey out or drop cells with n < 10 in heatmaps (F12, F13, F21, F22, F25, F26); remove decimal places from percentages built on n < 30.

**M10. Text/table inconsistencies on headline numbers.**
- "Seventeen of the twenty differences survive Holm correction" (Exec summary 4, §5.1, paper): Table 8's own `significant_holm_05` column has 19 True and 1 False (only accountability fails). Recomputed: 19.
- "7 ... legally binding, against 301 and 29" (Exec summary 1, Conclusion 1, paper abstract) uses `doc_class`; §3.2 "39 (14.4%)" and the chi-square use `binding_nature`. The 10 extra are 9 bills annotated `legally_binding` while `draft_or_bill_under_consideration` (a codebook inconsistency: a bill is not binding) plus D0227.
- "Regions with fewer than 20 embodied documents excluded" (§6) vs code threshold >= 15 (Oceania, n = 16, is included).
- "Only 32.9% of embodied documents cite any named instrument" is after canonicalisation to 29 instruments (81% of reference strings map); the raw shares are 37.5% vs 53.7%.
- Exec summary 3 says medical robots "sit near parity" (gap -7.7); §4.4 lists surgical robots among those receiving "proportionally more instrument attention".
- Fix: regenerate the narrative numbers from the tables and choose one binding definition.

### MINOR

**m1. The stated Fisher rule is not what the code does (§2 step 7; `analysis_common.chi2_or_fisher`).** Fisher is applied only to 2x2 tables with an *observed* count < 5; the text says "where expected counts are small". The binding-nature 2x3 test has minimum expected count 4.0 and was run as chi-square (p = 0.0029); a permutation test gives p = 0.005, so the conclusion stands, but Table 15 contains r x 2 tables with up to 8 cells of expected count < 5.

The embodied-specific principles are near-tautologically embodied-only (Finding 4: "62.6% vs 0.2%").** A document scored on human-robot proximity or liability for physical damage will almost always carry an embodied-category label and therefore be in the embodied set by definition. **m7. 2026 is 289 usable documents (18%) and extends to October, yet enters every trend as a full year (F02, F05, F14, F33, Table 16, Table 17).** Say "to October 2026" in each caption; show the trend models with 2026 excluded (see M1).

Figure 23's x-axis labels overprint one another; Figure 34's legend sits on top of the "Ethics principles" and "HRI" data points; Figure 31's dendrogram labels overprint the heatmap row labels; Figure 2's "Other AI governance" includes non-AI documents.

Undelivered items from the registered analysis plan (`report/stage2_analysis_plan.md`).** Per-capita/per-document normalisation (A4), community detection (B7), pairwise regional comparisons (D17), negative-binomial count models, and re-running results under the alternate annotator (F20) are promised and absent, without a note.

## 3. What is done well (keep)

- Full scripted pipeline with a single results store (`data/results.json`), a CSV twin for every figure and table, prompt hash and cost reporting; every number I checked reproduces exactly.
- A codebook written before annotation, with an explicit 0/1/2 salience scale and embodied-specific principles; the three-way scope field (`ai_scope`) and `relevance` score make the set definition auditable (which is how M5 was found).
- Wilson intervals with n labels in Figures 5 and 7; Holm correction within families; effect sizes alongside p-values; the composition effect in Figure 5 is correctly called a collection artefact.
- The instrument-only columns in Table 10 show the right instinct; they just need the tests and need extending to principles.
- Part F is unusually honest for a report of this kind: sensitivity table across eleven subsets, coverage figure, flagged ISO landing pages, test-retest as well as inter-model reliability, a lexicon cross-check against full text.
- Multi-key deduplication (hash, URL, title, prefix, MinHash) and the document-recovery step are good practice even though the title pass needs widening.
- Clear definitions in §1 and a readable narrative that always names the table or figure behind a claim.

## 4. Additional analyses that would materially strengthen the study (ranked by value per unit cost)

1. **Genre-stratified re-analysis of every embodied-vs-general comparison** (principles, mechanisms, sectors, citations): logistic models with set + doc_class + region, or CMH, with research-only and instrument-only panels. Low cost (one afternoon on existing tables); changes Findings 4, 6 and the conclusions.
2. **Merge duplicates and recompute all instrument counts** (301/63/381/7, Figure 6, Tables 2, 7, 13). Low cost; removes an embarrassing error class.
3. **Instrument-level claims only on substantive captures**: exclude landing/partial pages (or re-capture ISO scope texts) before Table 9/Figure 23 and the standards columns of S12/S13. Low-medium cost.
4. **Composition-adjusted period and trend models**: period dummies, doc_class + region covariates, Firth-penalised logistic, with/without 2026. Low cost; fixes C4 and M1.
5. **Human validation sample** (100-150 documents, stratified by class and set) for set membership, binding nature, level-2 embodied principles and mechanisms; report human-vs-LLM kappa alongside LLM-vs-LLM. Medium cost; converts "reliability" into "validity".
6. **Re-run the headline tables on the 160-document sample under the Opus annotations** (plan item F20). Low cost; already promised.
7. **Uncertainty for the geometry**: bootstrap CIs on cosine distances and split-half noise floors; bootstrap or Bayesian intervals on the gap index; a primary-category version of the gap index. Low cost.
8. **Sensitivity to the relevance threshold**: all headline numbers with relevance >= 2 only (drops 120 documents) and with `general_ai_with_embodied_provisions` as a third group. Low cost.
9. **Exact tests and bias-corrected V for Table 15**, restricted to embodied instruments where the question is about embodied AI (and reported as counts where n < 10). Low cost.
10. **Chunked full-text annotation for governance instruments** (the documents that matter most are the longest: AI Act 97k words read at 14k characters). Medium-high cost; the lexicon check suggests excerpt-based reading under-detects content in long instruments.
11. **Coverage-weighted or per-capita normalisation** (plan item A4) and a sprint/database-stratified estimate, so that "Europe 43.7%" and country rankings are read against what the collectors listed rather than what they captured. Medium cost.
12. **Order-free instrument canonicalisation** (anchored regexes, longest-match-first, manual table for the top 100 reference strings). Low cost.
