# Adversarial review: figures and interactive dashboard

Reviewer role: data-visualization specialist / front-end QA. Scope: the 34 light figures (`figures/F*.png`), 5 dark variants, their CSV twins, `scripts/figstyle.py`, and `dashboard/index.html` (generated by `scripts/10_dashboard.py`). Reference rules: the dataviz skill's `anti-patterns.md`, `marks-and-anatomy.md`, `palette.md`; validator `validate_palette.js`. Dashboard tested with headless Chromium (Playwright) at 1280x900 and 400x800; counts cross-checked against `data/master_table.csv` with pandas. ## 1. Verdict (10 lines)

1. The figure set is stylistically coherent and honest at the data level: 8 of 8 figure-vs-CSV cross-checks (F05, F07, F11, F18, F26, F28, F29, F35) reproduce the plotted values exactly; no dual axes, rainbows, dashed grids or value-ramps on nominal categories anywhere.
2. The palette in `figstyle.py` is hex-for-hex the reference palette and passes the validator in both modes (one expected WARN: aqua/yellow/magenta are below 3:1 on the light surface, so they need relief).
3. Three figures have outright label collisions that make them unfit to publish as-is: F23 (x-tick labels overprint each other), F34 (legend drawn on top of the two bottom data rows), F37 (legend covers the Europe bar). F31 prints region names across the dendrogram branches.
4. Every dark-mode heatmap (10 figures) is mis-encoded: the ramp runs surface(L=0.01) -> light blue (L=0.54) -> navy (L=0.08), so the lowest and highest values are both dark and the midrange is lightest; light ink on the mid-blue cells sits at 1.55-1.84:1.
5. Small-n percentages are presented with the same visual weight as large-n ones (F27 draws a 100% bar for n=2; F25's darkest cells come from Africa n=17; F23 shows 43% of n=7); the small-n regions/classes that were dropped are never named.
6. Captions over-promise in two places (F23 "mentioned and operationalised" plots only level 2; F24 "and for governance instruments only" plots only all-documents shares), F23 has no CSV twin and F31's twin has a different name, contradicting the report's "every figure has a CSV twin" claim.
7. The dashboard's logic is correct: filters, tiles, four charts and the table stay consistent (Europe + research output + 2020-2024 -> 200 in the UI, 200 in pandas; chart totals 200/200), colours are bound to the entity (no recolour-on-filter), every chart has a table twin, load is 0.55 s for 3.47 MB, zero console errors.
8. The dashboard file is not a complete HTML document: no doctype (renders in quirks mode), no `<meta charset>` (1 of 10 headless loads decoded it as windows-1252 and rendered mojibake everywhere, screenshots 01/05/06), no viewport meta (a real phone lays it out at 980 px), no `lang`.
9. On a 400 px phone the SVG charts scale down to 6.2 px text and 10 px bars, and the sticky filter bar is 409 px tall (51% of the viewport); on desktop the end-value of the longest bar is clipped in 3 of 4 charts ("706" -> "70", "228" -> "22", "117" -> "11") and 8 row labels are truncated with an ellipsis.
10. Keyboard access is partial: table rows open with Enter (not Space), column sorting and chart tooltips are mouse-only, and the detail drawer has no dialog semantics, focus move or focus trap. The data itself leaks two impossible years into the hero tiles ("Year span 1594-2027").

## 2. Per-figure review (all 34 light figures)

Severity: **High** = misleads or unreadable; **Medium** = clear rule violation that costs clarity; **Low** = polish; **OK** = nothing material.

Systemic findings that apply to many figures (referenced as S1-S9 in the table):

- **S1 Dark-mode sequential ramp is non-monotonic.** `analysis_common.heatmap()` builds the colormap as `[surface] + SEQ[2:11]`. On the dark surface that is L 0.010 -> 0.537 -> 0.080 (WCAG relative luminance), so value 0 and the maximum are both dark while ~30% of max is the lightest cell. The annotation ink is the light text colour in dark mode, giving 1.55:1 on `#9ec5f4` and 1.84:1 on `#86b6ef` (verified on F12_dark, F16_dark, F31_dark). Affects F12, F13, F14, F16, F17, F21, F22, F25, F30, F31 dark variants. Fix: in dark mode run the ramp surface -> SEQ steps reversed (dark navy first, light blue last) so lightness rises with value, and pick the in-cell ink by cell luminance, not by `v > 0.55*max`.
- **S2 Zero cells vanish and vmin floats.** Cells at the minimum are painted in the surface colour, so "0" cells read as holes (F12, F13, F21, F22, F25, F30, F31 diagonal). Because `vmin` is not fixed, the lightest step means 0 in F12/F13 but 2 in F14 and 4 in F21; the three category heatmaps also use different maxima (43/50/43), so shades are not comparable across the figures. Fix: `vmin=0`, a shared `vmax` within a family, and a visible lightest step (`SEQ[0]`, not the surface).
- **S3 Slug labels.** `nice()` turns codebook slugs into prose by replacing underscores, producing "Elder care social care", "Public administration justice", "Safety physical harm", "Human robot interaction social effects", "Prohibitions bans", "Licensing registration", "Ngo or civil society". Fix: a label dictionary like `PRL`/`DCL` for sectors, topics, mechanisms and issuer types.
- **S4 Titles describe, never state the message.** 34 of 34 titles are of the form "X by Y"; the one near-exception is F18. Fix: title = finding, subtitle = what is plotted.
- **S5 Low-contrast slots used for large fills and thin lines.** Yellow `#eda100` (2.11:1), magenta `#e87ba4` (2.62:1) and aqua `#1baf7a` (2.74:1) carry whole segments in F01/F04/F10/F26/F27 and a line in F06. White text inside magenta is 2.69:1 and inside aqua 2.82:1 (F01 "76%", F26 "60%", F10 "31%"). Relief exists (in-segment percentages above a threshold, CSV twins) but the dominant category of the whole corpus, research output, lives on the weakest slot.
- **S6 Small n presented at full weight.** Percent heatmap columns with n=12-17 (F13/F22 Proposal 12, Grey 13; F25 Oceania 15, Africa 17; F12/F21 Oceania 16), F23 Binding n=7, F26 Oceania n=14, F27 Proposal n=2. Groups below the inclusion threshold are dropped silently (F12/F21/F26/F31 drop Latin America, Africa, Canada; F13/F22 drop Binding n=7). Fix: a minimum-n rule stated in the subtitle, hatched or greyed small-n cells, and "excluded: ..." in the caption.
- **S7 Region naming and order vary.** Short names (`RSHORT`: "USA", "Canada", "Latin America") in F07-F31; sheet names ("North America - USA", "Latin America - Caribbean") and sheet order in F37; the dashboard uses the long names and sorts by count. "Canada" hides Greenland.
- **S8 Hue does not follow the entity across the report.** Blue/orange are reassigned per figure: embodied/general (F03, F07, F19, F20, F24, F29), research/instruments (F18), recent/old (F34), model/retest (F35), sprint/database (F37). Research output is pink in F01/F04/F13 but blue in F18. Acceptable per figure (each has a legend) but a reader moving through the report has to relearn the key every page.
- **S9 CSV twin gaps.** F23 has no CSV; F31's twin is `F31_region_distances.csv` while the PNG is `F31_region_similarity` (and the dendrogram leaf order is not in the CSV). The report's line 25 claims every figure has a twin.

The file starts with `<title>` and a `claude:viewport-fit-sides` meta, i.e. it was written to be wrapped by the artifact publisher, but it ships as `dashboard/index.html`.
- Consequence 1: on 1 of 10 headless loads Chromium's sniffer chose windows-1252 and every non-ASCII character rendered as mojibake: tile "1594â€“2027", eyebrow "CEI AI ROBOTICS CORPUS Â· DOCUMENT", labels "North America (Canada, Grâ€¦", titles "RecuperaciÃ³n", "TÃ¼rkiye", drawer "United Kingdom (GB) Â· Europe" (screenshots `01_initial_1280.png`, `05_drawer_1280.png`, `06_dark_1280.png`). - Consequence 2: with mobile emulation (`isMobile: true`, 400 px) the page lays out at 980 px and is scaled down (`08_phone400_mobiletrue.png`: scrollWidth 980).
- Fix: emit `<!doctype html><html lang="en"><head><meta charset="utf-8"><meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width,initial-scale=1">...` from `10_dashboard.py` (keep the artifact meta; the wrapper tolerates a full document), or document that the file must only be viewed through the publisher.

### D2 (High) Impossible years reach the hero tiles and the year chart
- Evidence: tile "Year span 1594–2027" (`01_initial_1280.png`); first row of the default (year desc) sort is D1462 "Ergonomics, Values and Robot Ethics", year 2027; year chart has a "2027" bar; D1446 "The Rape of Lucrece" (1594) and D0756 (1947 Japanese antimonopoly act) are in the "<2010" bucket. `year_source` is "annotation" for all three.

### D3 (High) End-value labels are clipped at the right edge in 3 of 4 charts
- Evidence: chart 1 Europe reads "70" (706), chart 3 Social companion robots "22" (228), chart 4 Beneficence "11" (117) and Non-maleficence "11" (115) (`01_initial_1280.png`, right edge of each card). `hbars()` places the total at `x+4` of a 600-unit viewBox with only 16 units of right margin.

### D4 (High, mobile) Charts and filters do not adapt to 400 px

No page-level horizontal overflow (scrollWidth 400) because the 1,100 px table scrolls inside its wrapper, which is correct.
- Fix: make the filter bar a collapsible disclosure (or non-sticky) below 860 px; render SVG text at a fixed CSS size (compute the viewBox width from the container, or use HTML/flex bars); 2-up tiles with a smaller value size.

### D5 (Medium) Keyboard and assistive-technology gaps
- Evidence: `th` sort headers are not focusable (`tabIndex -1`, 0 of 12 tabbable), so sorting is mouse-only; rows open with Enter but not Space; after Enter the drawer opens (`classList` "open") but `document.activeElement` stays on the row, the drawer has `aria-label` only (no `role="dialog"`, no `aria-modal`, no focus trap or return, background still scrolls); 0 chart rects are focusable, so tooltips are hover-only (the table views mitigate this); the active sort column is indicated by colour only (`th.on`), no arrow; the relevance column shows a 38 px bar whose value exists only in a `title` tooltip (tooltip-gated value); no skip link; `<svg role="img" aria-label>` is present (good).

### D6 (Medium) Year chart bars breach the 24 px cap and fill the slot

### D9 (Medium) Table chrome defects

### D10 (Low) Robustness and hygiene

## 4. Palette validator output and verdict

`scripts/figstyle.py` defines `CAT_LIGHT`, `CAT_DARK`, `SEQ` (13 steps 100-700), `DIV_BLUE/DIV_RED`, and the chrome tokens; the dashboard CSS repeats the same hex values as `--s1..--s8`, `--seq2/--seq5`, `--grid`, `--axis`, `--ring`. Every value is identical to `references/palette.md` (categorical slots in the July-2026 order, light slot 7 `#6250d6`, surfaces `#fcfcfb`/`#1a1a19`, page `#f9f9f7`/`#0d0d0d`, inks, gridline, baseline, diverging midpoints). Verdict: the palette itself is correct and needs no change. The problems are in how it is applied: (a) the WARN is real in practice because yellow, magenta and aqua carry large fills (F01/F04/F10/F26/F27, dashboard research-output class) and a thin line (F06) with relief only via in-segment labels above a threshold and the CSV twins; (b) `heatmap()` composes the sequential ramp from the surface plus `SEQ[2:11]` and reuses it unchanged in dark mode, which breaks lightness monotonicity on the dark surface (S1); (c) the dashboard's dark `--seq2 #184f95` is 2.15:1 on the dark surface, at the floor for an ordinal step; (d) `figstyle.py` has no helper for in-fill label ink (each script hard-codes `"#0b0b0b" if j==3 else "#ffffff"`, which is right for yellow but gives 2.69:1 white-on-magenta); (e) `lines.linewidth` is 1.8 rather than the 2 px spec and `grid.linewidth` 0.6 (both harmless). Figures use DejaVu Sans, the dashboard Public Sans: two different sans faces across one report.
