# Adversarial methods review: "Governing machines that act in the world" (embodied-AI governance study)

Reviewer role: AI researcher (LLM-based annotation, computational social science, measurement validity).
Scope: codebook, annotation pipeline, reliability and validation design, analysis sets, and the claims built on them. All numbers below were recomputed read-only from `annotations/*`, `data/master_table.csv`, `data/registry.csv`, the source texts and `scripts/*`; the recomputation commands are summarised in Appendix A so each can be re-run.

---

## 1. Overall verdict

1. The study is an ambitious, well-engineered LLM-annotation pipeline with a pre-written 39-field codebook, schema-enforced output, stored raw outputs with cost, two model-based reliability designs, collector cross-checks, a full-text lexicon check and an 11-subset sensitivity table. That is more validation effort than most LLM-annotation papers in this area attempt.
2. It nevertheless has **no human validation of any field**, and the two model-based checks are between sibling models of the same vendor that both received the same leaked metadata; agreement therefore bounds consistency, not accuracy.
3. The reliability figures in the paper (median kappa 0.89, test-retest 0.95) do not describe the annotations actually analysed: 54 of the 160 reliability documents were replaced by re-cleaned re-annotations, and a same-model re-annotation of 499 documents that exists in the repository shows far lower stability (page quality kappa 0.57, date basis 0.41, stance 0.63, relevance 0.78, publication year changed for 19% of documents) and is not reported.
4. The model saw a median of 33% of the text of truncated documents (58% of the corpus; 51 of the 63 embodied instruments; 2.3% of the EU AI Act), and the examples show it filling the gap from parametric knowledge, which the codebook forbids.
5. The full-text lexicon "independent check" contains regex bugs that explain its own worst results: `\bLAWS\b` compiled case-insensitively matches "laws" (371 of 463 military-lexicon documents fire only on that), `\bharm` matches "harmonised", "defen[cs]e" matches "Defesa do Consumidor".
6. The headline "embodied principle profile" is partly tautological (six "embodied-specific principles" are definitionally about physical systems) and, for the three core principles the paper names (non-maleficence, dignity, freedom), the lexicon measure does not reproduce the direction of the difference.
7. The headline instrument counts are fragile: EO 14110 is among the "7 binding embodied instruments" only because a re-annotation flipped its scope; the AI Act is counted twice; 27 landing or catalogue pages are counted as governance documents (the paper says three).
8. Trend claims (2025–26 "turn to industrial policy") are largely a genre-composition shift plus capture-date inference; within research outputs the rise is +4 points, not +13.
9. Reproducibility is partial: no version control, the exact model input per document is not stored and the cleaning function changed between runs under an unchanged prompt hash.
10. Recommendation: **major revision**. The dataset and pipeline are salvageable and valuable; the paper should not claim "reliable" or "validated" annotation until a human gold standard, a metadata-blind and full-text re-annotation, and corrected lexicons exist, and the framing ("first quantitative map") should be narrowed.

## 2. Findings ranked by severity

Severity key: **Critical** = undermines a central claim or the validation story; **Major** = materially changes a reported number or its interpretation; **Minor** = should be fixed, limited effect on conclusions.

### Critical

#### C1. Model–model agreement is presented as validation, but both annotators are same-vendor siblings that received the same leaked labels; no human validation exists
- **Location:** `report/paper.md` §2.3, §4.2, §5.6, abstract ("Reliability was assessed against an independent model"); `scripts/04_annotate.py` `build_input()`; `scripts/09_reliability.py`.
- **Problem:** (a) Claude Sonnet 5.5 and Claude Opus 5.5 share training data, post-training and instruction-following style; their agreement measures shared bias as much as truth. (b) The prompt's METADATA block passes `region_folder`, `collector_title`, `source_url`, `detected_language` and `text_status` to both models, and the crawler-generated header inside the text itself contains the collectors' labels (`**Issuer / country:** …`, `**Type:** Intergovernmental body`, `**Dr. K rubric tier:** Supporting`). The highest-kappa fields are exactly those with leaked inputs (country 0.97, language 0.94, issuer type 0.93), and the "cross-check against collectors' labels" (kappa 0.95 on issuer type, 95% on country) is not independent either.
- **Evidence:** 479 of 1,613 usable texts (all in the Europe folder: 479 of 707) carry the header; 473 of the 692 Europe documents that have a collector `Type` label have that label verbatim in the text the model read, i.e. ~70% of the n=679 population used for the issuer-type cross-check. 46 of the 160 reliability documents carry it. The codebook says "Code only what the text and metadata support", so the models were instructed to use the leaked labels. Example header (D1060): `**Issuer / country:** Europe-wide (EU and other European bodies)  **Type:** Intergovernmental body  **Dr. K rubric tier:** Supporting`.
- **Fix:** Strip crawler headers and remove `region_folder`, `collector_title`, `detected_language` and `text_status` from the prompt (keep URL/filename only if the field logically needs them, and say so). Re-run the 160-document sample metadata-blind. Add a human expert gold standard (see §4, E1) and report model-vs-human kappa per field. Describe Opus as a "second model from the same family", not "independent".

#### C2. The reported reliability does not describe the analysed annotations, and an unreported same-model re-annotation shows much lower stability
- **Location:** `scripts/09_reliability.py` (loads `annotations/main` as the reference), `scripts/07_assemble.py` (uses `annotations/main_clean` for 499 docs), `report/paper.md` §4.2/§5.6, `report/supplement.md` S4.
- **Problem:** Both reliability comparisons are against the *original* `annotations/main` outputs. For 499 documents (29%), the master table instead uses the re-cleaned re-annotation in `annotations/main_clean`. 54 of the 160 reliability documents are among them, so a third of the reliability sample refers to annotations no longer in the analysis. More importantly, the main-vs-main_clean pair is a same-model, same-prompt, perturbed-input retest that the paper does not report, and it is far less reassuring than the reported test-retest (median 0.95).
- **Evidence (recomputed on the 499 overlap):** kappa page_quality 0.57, date_basis 0.41, stance 0.63, risk_framing 0.79, relevance 0.78, pub_year agreement 81% (changed for 96 documents), doc_type 0.83, binding_nature 0.82, ai_scope 0.85; principles quadratic-weighted median 0.81 (min 0.75); governance_topics mean per-label 0.76 (exact-set match 44%); governance_mechanisms 0.73; embodied-set membership flipped for 15 documents. Concrete case: D0915 (EO 14110): original = `general_ai_only`, no categories, `superseded_or_repealed`, date inferred; re-annotation = `general_ai_with_embodied_provisions`, three categories, `published_current`, date stated. The paper's list of "7 binding embodied instruments" includes EO 14110 only because of the second run.
- **Fix:** Recompute reliability against the final annotations (and state which run each reliability document comes from). Report the main-vs-main_clean comparison as an input-perturbation sensitivity result alongside test-retest. State that same-model test-retest within 45 minutes on identical input measures sampling noise only.

#### C3. Excerpt truncation: the model saw a minority of most long documents, and the affected fields are the ones the paper's instrument-level claims depend on; the gap is filled from parametric knowledge
- **Location:** `scripts/04_annotate.py` `excerpt()` (10,000 + 2,500 + 1,500 chars); `report/paper.md` §4.2, §7.
- **Problem:** 14,000 characters is roughly 2,500–3,500 words. Governance instruments put their operative content (mechanisms, conformity procedures, annexes listing products, entry-into-force clauses, references to other instruments) in the body and the end, not the first 10,000 characters. The lexicon check cannot detect the loss (see M1). The codebook forbids coding beyond the text, yet the annotations clearly rely on world knowledge for well-known instruments, which creates a fame bias (famous instruments get richer, knowledge-based codes; obscure ones get excerpt-limited codes).
- **Evidence:** 984 of 1,703 documents (57.8%) exceed 14,500 cleaned characters; among usable documents ≥300 words it is 62%. For truncated documents the median fraction of characters seen is 33% (p25 18%, p10 8.5%). Among the 63 embodied governance instruments, 51 are truncated, median fraction seen 41%; the EU AI Act D1291 (604,643 chars) was seen at 2.3%, yet is coded with 15 mechanisms and 5 embodied categories (Annex I products are not in the first 10,000 characters); EO 14110 D0915 at 10%; WHO health guidance D0104 at 2.9%; IEEE Ethically Aligned Design D1008 at 2.2%. D0915's original annotation gives `superseded_or_repealed`; the captured text contains no "rescind", "revoke" or "repeal" (only "frozen in time"): the status came from the model's knowledge that the order was rescinded in January 2025. Fields most exposed: governance_mechanisms, named_instruments_referenced, principle level 2 ("dedicated section"), instrument_status, countries_discussed, embodied_categories for general-AI acts (annexes), key_provisions.
- **Fix:** Annotate governance instruments (n=381) and the reliability sample on full text (chunk-and-merge above the context limit); record `fraction_seen` per document and use it as a covariate/stratifier; add an explicit instruction "if the status/provision is not in the excerpt, code `unknown`" and audit whether the model complies; report which fields change between excerpt and full-text runs (§4, E2).

### Major

#### M1. The full-text lexicons contain regex bugs that account for their own worst results; the "independent check" is not interpretable as published
- **Location:** `scripts/05_lexicons.py` (`pat={k:re.compile(v,re.I)}`), `codebook/lexicons.json`, Table 18 / Figure 36, `report/analysis_report.md` §9.2.
- **Problem:** All patterns are compiled case-insensitively, so acronym alternatives match ordinary words, and several stems are far too broad.
- **Evidence (recomputed on 1,613 texts):** `lex_military_laws` contains `\bLAWS\b`; 371 of the 463 documents it flags match *only* through the word "laws" — this, not "passing references to autonomous weapons" (analysis report §9.2), is why the lexicon fires on 29.1% of documents and kappa is 0.14. `lex_p_safety` (used for non-maleficence) contains `\bharm`; 1,519 of its 6,008 "harm" hits are "harmoni-" (EU "harmonised rules"), and 115 documents fire only through "harmonis-". `lex_p_dual_use` contains `defen[cs]e|militar|arma`; 289 of its 501 documents fire only on that family (5 via "Defesa/Defensa del Consumidor"), inflating dual-use "prevalence" to 31.7%. `lex_humanoid` matches "Android" (the OS, 5 documents); `lex_p_autonomy` contains `manipulat` which matches "manipulator" (49 documents in a robotics corpus); `lex_oecd_principles` fires on any "OCDE"; `lex_p_privacy` fires on cookie-banner "personal data" (in short non-truncated documents the lexicon is present but the annotator absent for 33% of documents); `innovation`, `environmental`, `inclusion`, `benefit` are near-universal in policy text.
- **Fix:** Use case-sensitive patterns for acronyms (`LAWS`, `UAS`, `AMR`, `AGV`, `ISO`, `IEC`), add negative look-aheads (`harm(?!oni)`), restrict dual-use to weapon/dual-use terms, validate each lexicon's precision on 30 random hits and report it, then recompute Table 18, Figure 36 and the Spearman correlations.

#### M2. The Spearman "0.93" is misdescribed, and the lexicon measure contradicts specific principle claims in the text
- **Location:** `scripts/15_analysis_C.py` lines 34–37; `report/paper.md` §4.3, §5.6, abstract; `report/analysis_report.md` executive summary item 10, §9.2.
- **Problem:** The code correlates the embodied-minus-general *differences* in principle presence (rho = 0.93); the paper says it correlates principle *shares* ("the ranking of principles by annotated share … with the ranking by lexicon share"). The share correlation is 0.84. The difference correlation is dominated by the six embodied-specific principles, whose lexicons are robot vocabulary and therefore co-vary with set membership by construction.
- **Evidence (recomputed, n=1,551):** annotated vs lexicon embodied-minus-general differences: dignity +0.12 vs −0.02; human_oversight +0.08 vs −0.03; non_maleficence +0.12 vs +0.01; freedom_autonomy +0.13 vs +0.10 (supported); physical_safety +0.46 vs +0.11; human_robot_proximity +0.63 vs +0.25; autonomy_level_control +0.36 vs +0.03; liability_for_physical_damage +0.23 vs +0.06. The paper's sentence "embodied documents … also invoke non-maleficence, dignity and freedom more often" is supported by the lexicon for one of the three, and the embodied-specific differences are 3–10× larger in annotation than in text vocabulary.
- **Fix:** Correct the description; report rho for shares and for differences, for the 14 core principles separately (shares 0.80, differences 0.93); show per-principle annotated vs lexicon differences in a table; remove or heavily caveat the dignity/non-maleficence claim.

#### M3. Set definition is circular with the outcomes, and the embodied-vs-general comparison is a genre comparison
- **Location:** `scripts/analysis_common.py` `load()`; `report/paper.md` §3.3, §5.3, §6.
- **Problem:** The embodied set is defined by the model's own `relevance ≥ 2` OR any model-assigned `embodied_categories`, and the outcomes (principles, mechanisms, topics) come from the same model call. Any internal association in the model's reading (e.g. "robots" → "physical safety") appears as a between-set difference. The six "embodied-specific principles" (physical safety, proximity, physical liability, autonomy level, conformity assessment, dual use) are definitionally about physical systems, so "62.6% vs 0.2%" is a consistency check on the annotator, not a finding. The embodied set is 75.5% research output, the general set is instrument- and communication-heavy; the paper concedes this in one clause ("partly comparisons of genres") but keeps the pooled comparisons as headline findings.
- **Evidence:** `embodied_set = (relevance>=2) | (embodied_categories != "")` & `ai_scope != not_ai` (analysis_common.py line 40); 6 documents enter via category labels alone, including the Finnish AI Act (D1060, relevance 1). Sonnet–Opus kappa on set membership is 0.91 (good consistency), but no external criterion is ever used.
- **Fix:** Define the embodied set from a criterion outside the annotation (collector sprint set, lexicon robot density, or human screening of a sample) and show the overlap; stratify every embodied-vs-general comparison by `doc_class`; present the six embodied-specific items as topics, not principles, and exclude them from the "profile" claim.

#### M4. The "7 binding embodied instruments" headline (and its chi-square) is fragile and partly an artefact
- **Location:** abstract, `report/analysis_report.md` executive summary item 2, §9.4, Table S01.
- **Problem / Evidence:** Of the 7: D0915 (EO 14110) is in the set only because of the re-annotation (C2) and at relevance 1; D1060 (Finnish AI Act, relevance 1, `partial_or_excerpt`) enters via category labels alone and duplicates D1291; only 4 have relevance ≥ 2 and only 3 are relevance 3 (El Salvador, Dubai, Taiwan). The chi-square on binding nature (p = 0.003, V = 0.19) treats the duplicate as two observations. "Only one of these is a general robotics statute" is right, but the denominator is unstable.
- **Fix:** Count unique instruments (merge language versions); report the relevance-3 definition as primary and the OR rule as sensitivity; drop the chi-square or compute it on unique instruments with Fisher's exact test.

#### M5. Residual duplication far beyond "the EU AI Act appears twice"
- **Location:** `scripts/01_build_registry.py` dedup; `report/paper.md` §3.2, §7.
- **Evidence:** 66 usable documents share an exact `title_en` with another document: 12 copies of "Commission publishes guidelines on AI system definition…" (language versions of one press release), 4 "Apply AI Strategy", 2 "A pro-innovation approach to AI regulation", 2 "Canada's National AI Strategy: AI for All", ISO/IEC TR 24028 twice (D1231/D1232), the Israeli high-tech strategy twice (D0191/D1687). MinHash on prose shingles cannot match translations.
- **Fix:** Add a dedup pass on normalised `title_en` + issuer + year (manual review of hits), or cluster language versions under one instrument id; recompute counts and tests with one row per instrument (or cluster-robust tests).

#### M6. Quadratic-weighted kappa and the mixed-definition "median over 46 fields" overstate reliability; the level that carries the operationalisation claims is the least reliable
- **Location:** `scripts/09_reliability.py`; abstract ("median Cohen's kappa 0.89 over 46 fields"), §5.6 ("41 fields above 0.80").
- **Evidence (Sonnet vs Opus, n=160):** principles quadratic-weighted median 0.89, unweighted 0.81, presence (>0) 0.85, substantive level (=2) 0.81 with solidarity 0.49, dignity 0.60, non-maleficence 0.63, justice 0.73, physical_safety 0.73, autonomy_level_control 0.79 (on 6 vs 4 positives). Relevance: quadratic 0.89, unweighted 0.71, percent agreement 79%. If principles are reported unweighted, the 46-field median is 0.83 and 34 (not 41) fields reach 0.80. The paper's "operationalised in fewer than one in twelve" claims rest on level 2.
- **Fix:** Report unweighted, presence and level-2 kappas side by side (or Krippendorff's alpha, ordinal), and state in the abstract that weighted and multi-label kappas are mixed in the median. Re-examine claims that depend on level 2 of solidarity, dignity, non-maleficence and autonomy-level control.

#### M7. Mean per-label kappa hides document-level disagreement and annotator-specific biases that matter for the bundle and distance analyses
- **Location:** `scripts/09_reliability.py` MULTI block; mechanism bundles (k-means) and regional cosine distances in `14_analysis_B.py`.
- **Evidence:** exact-set agreement Sonnet vs Opus: governance_topics 31%, governance_mechanisms 42%, application_sectors 69%, embodied_categories 82%. Labels included in the mean: `none` (kappa 0.43; Sonnet assigns it 55 times, Opus 22), `other` topic (0.38; 8 vs 16), `audits_impact_assessment` (0.57; 40 vs 22), `standards_interoperability` (0.66), `research_science` sector (0.61). The ≥6-positives rule (sum over both annotators) admits labels with 3 positives each. The k-means bundles and cosine profiles use the document vectors, whose reliability is the exact-match rate, not the per-label mean.
- **Fix:** Report Krippendorff's alpha with MASI/Jaccard distance, exact-match and Jaccard per field, the full per-label table in the supplement, and exclude `none`/`other` from the mean. Re-run k-means and the regional distances on the Opus annotations and report whether the clusters/ordering replicate.

#### M8. The text-cleaning rule deletes dates, titles, headings and list markers from the start of documents
- **Location:** `scripts/04_annotate.py` `clean_text()`: lines with ≤4 words within the first 400 kept lines are dropped unless they end in `.:;` or start with `#`.
- **Evidence (applied to the stored texts):** D0915: the line `October 30, 2023` is dropped (the annotation then records `date_basis: inferred_from_content`). D0005: `DECRETO N° 340`, `LEY DE TECNOLOGÍAS ROBÓTICAS`, `CAPÍTULO I`, `Objeto`, `Definiciones`, `Ámbito de aplicación` and the list markers `a)`…`h)` are dropped. D1523: `Vol 23 (1), 2024`, `Received: 11 May 2024`, `1. Introduction`. D0003 (Brazilian humanoid bill): line-wrapped PDF text loses mid-sentence fragments of the ementa (`segurança,`, `transparência`, `robôs humanoides no território`). This plausibly contributes to 389 inferred dates (24.5% of usable documents) and to pub_year changing for 19% of documents between runs (C2).
- **Fix:** Apply navigation stripping only to HTML captures and only to lines matching nav patterns; whitelist dates, headings, numbering and lines in the first 50 lines; keep PDF structure; re-annotate affected documents and report how many dates become `stated_in_document`.

#### M9. Trend findings are confounded by genre composition and capture-date inference
- **Location:** `report/paper.md` §5.5, §6 ("The frontier is moving from ethics to control and industrial policy"); `report/analysis_report.md` §7, Table 16/17.
- **Evidence (embodied set, usable):** 2020–24 (n=337) is 82.2% research, 6.8% communication/news; 2025–26 (n=202) is 69.3% research, 12.4% communication/news, 10.4% instruments. `industrial_policy_innovation` is 76% in 2025–26 communication/news vs 12.9% in 2025–26 research; within research outputs the change is 8.7% → 12.9% (+4.2 points), not the pooled +13.3. Ethics within research 69.3 → 62.9; HRI 64.3 → 53.6. Year 2026 has 289 usable documents, 83 with inferred dates, 129 communication/news, 86 partial/landing captures; 16 are dated "October 2026", the capture month (e.g. D0398, an ISO Store landing page coded `technical_standard`, year 2026). 26.7% of 2025–26 documents have inferred dates.
- **Fix:** Run the period comparison and logistic trends stratified by `doc_class` (or with `doc_class` as a covariate); repeat excluding inferred dates and excluding 2026; state the within-research effect in the text; never infer a year from a capture date (instruct the model to return `null`).

#### M10. Landing and catalogue pages are counted as governance instruments; the paper understates this
- **Location:** `codebook/codebook.md` decision rule 3; `analysis_common.py` `usable` (does not exclude `landing_or_index_page`); `report/paper.md` §7 ("Three ISO catalogue pages were captured in place of standards").
- **Evidence:** 105 landing pages are in the usable set; 27 of them are `is_governance_document = True`, including 19 standards-catalogue pages (ISO/IEC, IEEE 2863, TC260), the AI Act EUR-Lex landing page (D1010), ISO/TC 299 and "ISO - Robotics" portal pages, and the Commission's draft high-risk guidelines page; 7 of the 63 embodied instruments and 13 more are `partial_or_excerpt`. D0398 (ISO Store) is `technical_standard`, `governance_instrument_soft`, `is_governance_document: false` (inconsistent), year 2026 inferred, embodied set via `industrial_manipulators`. Landing pages have mean 3.3 principles and 1.0 mechanisms, so they also dilute instrument-level rates.
- **Fix:** Exclude `landing_or_index_page` from instrument counts, mechanism rates and the operationalisation tables, or report them as "instrument references (metadata only)"; correct the limitation text; revise rule 3.

#### M11. "Regions converge on principles and diverge on mechanisms" is consistent with a ceiling/base-rate artefact and small regional samples
- **Location:** `report/paper.md` §5.4, §6; Table 15, Figure 31.
- **Evidence:** Principle *presence* (≥1 = "mentioned in passing") is near-saturated: non-maleficence 73%, beneficence 74%, mean 7.4 of 20 principles present per document, 30.5% of documents have ≥10. Cramér's V on a saturated binary is attenuated toward zero; mechanisms have base rates of 2–30% and more room to vary. Regional n in the embodied set: Middle East 37, Oceania 16, Latin America 14, Africa 11, Canada 9; "liability-led framing in 60% of Middle East embodied documents" is about 22 documents. Non-significance across small regions is reported as "convergence".
- **Fix:** Test regional differences on level-2 (substantive) principles, which are not saturated; report V with bootstrap CIs and n per region on the figure; phrase null results as "no detectable difference at this sample size".

#### M12. The confidence field is essentially uncalibrated and the "confidence ≥ 0.5" sensitivity subset is vacuous
- **Location:** `codebook/codebook.md` §F; Table 19 row "Confidence >= 0.5 only".
- **Evidence (n=160):** Spearman between Sonnet confidence and per-document single-field agreement with Opus = 0.20 (p = 0.01); with principle agreement = −0.12 (n.s.); within substantive documents 0.21 / −0.12. Mean confidence is a page-quality lookup (substantive 0.87, partial 0.75, landing 0.75, blocked 0.27) with values concentrated at 0.80/0.85/0.90 (1,324 of 1,703). The ≥0.5 filter removes 33 of 1,589 usable documents.
- **Fix:** Do not use confidence as a quality weight or sensitivity axis; if retained, ask for per-field confidence and calibrate it against the gold standard (reliability diagram, ECE).

#### M13. Codebook construct overlap across the three parallel taxonomies
- **Location:** `codebook/codebook.md` §E.
- **Problem:** The same construct appears as topic, principle and mechanism: `certification_conformity_assessment` (topic) / `conformity_assessment_certification` (principle) / `conformity_assessment_certification` (mechanism, identical name); `human_oversight_control` / `human_oversight` / `human_oversight_requirement`; `transparency_explainability` / `transparency` / `transparency_disclosure_labelling`; `risk_management_risk_tiers` / `risk_based_tiering`; `civil_liability` / `liability_for_physical_damage` / `liability_regime`. "Conformity assessment" and "dual use/military" are not principles in the Jobin/OECD sense; `trust` is both a topic (`trust_acceptance`) and a principle. One passage of text therefore produces up to three positive codes, and the "principle profile" and "mechanism profile" are not independent measurements. `relevance` level 1 conflates "general AI" with "passing mention of robots", mixing two dimensions (embodiment, governance-ness); 63 documents are `ai_scope = embodied_*` yet relevance < 2.
- **Fix:** Define the three families orthogonally (topic = subject matter; principle = normative value invoked; mechanism = regulatory design feature), move conformity assessment and dual use out of principles, split relevance into "embodiment" and "governance content" ratings.

#### M14. Framing, related work and limitations are inadequate for a research audience
- **Location:** `report/paper.md` §1, §2.3, §7, §8 ("first quantitative map").
- **Problem:** (a) Prior quantitative or systematic mappings exist and are not cited: the EU RoboLaw project's regulatory mapping (Palmerini et al. 2014, "Guidelines on Regulating Robotics"), Holder et al. (2016, CLSR) on robotics law, Salvini, Paez-Granados & Billard (2021) gap analysis of ISO 13482, Fosch-Villaronga and colleagues' surveys of robot safety standards, the OECD.AI policy observatory, the AlgorithmWatch AI Ethics Guidelines Global Inventory, the Stanford AI Index and IAPP legislation trackers. (b) §2.3 cites only the favourable LLM-annotation literature (Gilardi et al. 2023; Ziems et al. 2024) and omits the cautionary and methodological work that this design needs: Reiss (2023) on prompt sensitivity; Pangakis, Wolken & Fasching (2023) "Automated annotation with generative AI requires validation"; Ollion et al. (2024) on proprietary models and reproducibility; Egami, Hinck, Stewart & Wei (2023) design-based supervised learning for debiasing downstream estimates from LLM labels; Barrie, Palmer & Spirling (2024) on replication; Thalken et al. (2023) on LLM annotation of legal reasoning at the edge of human agreement; Törnberg (2024) best practices. (c) The limitations omit: no human validation; same-vendor annotators; metadata leakage; parametric-knowledge contamination; fraction of text seen; the main-vs-clean instability; multilingual duplicates; lexicon defects; genre confounding of trends; landing pages as instruments; stance (kappa 0.59) still used in the Discussion ("56% neutral, 31% for new regulation, 4% adapt").
- **Fix:** Reframe as "an LLM-assisted quantitative mapping of a purposive multilingual collection"; add the related work; rewrite limitations around measurement validity; remove or caveat conclusions that rest on stance and risk framing.

### Minor

#### m1. Reproducibility gaps
- **Location:** `scripts/04_annotate.py`, `report/supplement.md` S1, `scripts/run_final.sh`.
- **Evidence:** no version control (`.git` absent); the CLI version is not recorded (currently 2.1.314); `claude-sonnet-5-5` is an alias, not a dated snapshot; the CLI exposes no temperature or seed; `--effort` is unset (default unknown); `prompt_hash` covers system prompt + schema but not the excerpt policy, cleaning function or metadata layout, so the original run and the re-cleaned run share hash `8d4d83e909f0` despite different inputs; the exact text sent per document is not stored, so no annotation can be re-derived; `run_final.sh` omits annotation; the paper says 161 re-run documents, the data has 160; the 7-document pilot (hash `8fa172c1ecd5`) and the codebook change after it are undocumented; `data/reliability_ids.txt` has no generating script or seed.
- **Fix:** Store `input_sha256` and the input text per annotation; record CLI version, model snapshot, effort; put the repository under git; add the sampling script with seed.

#### m2. The reliability sample is not a random 10% of the corpus
- **Evidence:** all 160 are `text_usable` and usable (118 substantive, 31 partial, 11 landing); 75% English; 46% Europe folder; 90 sprint / 70 database. The 90 metadata-only annotations, 23 blocked pages with text and 3 non-text documents are excluded. Fine as a design, but state it and stratify.

#### m3. Internal consistency violations in the annotations
- **Evidence (usable set):** 31 of 120 `general_ai_with_embodied_provisions` have no embodied category; 8 instrument-class documents have `is_governance_document = false`; 4 `is_governance_document = true` documents are reports; 40 governance documents have `binding_nature = not_applicable` (the codebook has no value for bills); 5 documents list `none` together with other mechanisms; 4 have categories but `embodiment_status = none`; 82 have categories but `primary_embodied_category = none`.
- **Fix:** Add post-hoc consistency rules or schema `if/then` constraints; add a `proposed_binding` value; report violation counts.

#### m4. Stance and framing are used substantively despite being the least reliable fields
- **Evidence:** stance kappa 0.59 (Opus) / 0.63 (re-clean); risk_framing 0.78 / 0.79. §6 uses the stance distribution to argue "the research community has not engaged with" adapting existing law.
- **Fix:** Move to supplement or drop.

#### m5. Statistical independence and multiplicity
- **Evidence:** chi-square tests treat multilingual duplicates and 78 multi-team documents as independent; Holm is applied within families but ~10 families are tested; Landis–Koch verbal labels are used as if normative.
- **Fix:** One row per instrument; report CIs rather than verbal kappa labels; consider a single FDR across all reported tests.

#### m6. "110 jurisdictions" in the title overstates analytic coverage
- **Evidence:** 113 jurisdiction codes in the usable set, 29 singletons, only 54 with ≥5 documents.
- **Fix:** "documents from 110 jurisdictions", and show the long tail.

#### m7. The lexicon validation set is a positive-only, no-gold design computed on partial and landing pages
- **Evidence:** n=1,551 includes 340 partial and 105 landing captures where lexicons fire on navigation and cookie text; both sides of the comparison are noisy, so kappa ≈ 0.4 cannot be attributed to either; the "density ≥ 0.5 per 1,000 words" threshold is unmotivated and changes kappa by up to 0.3 (human_oversight 0.46 → 0.18).
- **Fix:** Restrict to substantive captures; report both thresholds and precision per lexicon after M1 fixes; treat the lexicon as a screening measure, not validation.

#### m8. Language field: the annotated language is probably right, but the detector disagreement flags are mostly detector errors and the agreement is not independent evidence
- **Evidence:** of 46 documents annotated `en` but detected otherwise (≥300 words), inspected cases are English documents with non-English mastheads (D0382 Bestuur, D0384 Nigeria strategy) or OCR garbage (D0289); `detected_language` was passed in the prompt, so the 0.94 kappa and 91% detector agreement are not independent. No evidence of systematic under-detection of non-English content was found; non-English documents have slightly higher mean confidence (0.82 vs 0.83) and a higher substantive-capture share.

## 3. What is done well

- A full codebook with decision rules written before annotation, enforced by a JSON schema with closed enumerations; raw outputs stored verbatim with model, prompt hash, usage and cost; total spend reported (119.60 USD, reconciles exactly with the log files).
- Two reliability designs (second model, same-model re-run) plus collector cross-checks and an attempt at an annotation-independent full-text measure; the weakest fields (stance, page quality) are named explicitly and readers are warned about them.
- An 11-subset sensitivity table, Wilson intervals, Holm correction, effect sizes, and explicit acknowledgement of the sprint-versus-database artefact, Europe/English over-representation, inferred dates and lower bounds on instrument counts.
- Multilingual lexicons that at least attempt coverage of 15+ scripts; a QA-flag file (`data/qa_flags.csv`) that surfaces language disagreements, year conflicts and irrelevant sprint documents for review.
- A scripted pipeline from registry to report with CSV twins for every figure and table, a data dictionary, and a results store (`data/results.json`), which made this review's recomputations straightforward.
- Sensible codebook choices in places: genre-before-content rule, `software_agent_called_robot`, explicit handling of catalogue pages (even if the analytic consequence was not followed through), 0/1/2 salience scoring, and a named-instrument canonical list.
- Qualitative reading of the annotations shows the model producing accurate, specific summaries and key provisions where the excerpt contains them (D0005, D0228, D0003).

## 4. Additional validation experiments, ranked by credibility gained per unit cost

Cost assumptions: Sonnet 5.5 ≈ 0.043 USD per excerpt annotation (observed), Opus 5.5 ≈ 0.098 USD; full-text inputs at roughly 4 characters per token and ≈3 USD per million input tokens.

1. **Human expert gold standard with adjudication (highest priority).** Stratified sample of 120–200 documents (by doc_class × region × language × truncation), two domain coders (law/policy + robotics) on the ~15 fields that carry claims (doc_class, is_governance_document, instrument_status, binding_nature, ai_scope, embodied_categories, relevance, 6 embodied-specific items, 4 core principles at levels 1/2, governance_mechanisms), disagreements adjudicated. Report model-vs-human kappa per field, and then use design-based supervised learning / prediction-powered inference (Egami et al. 2023; Angelopoulos et al. 2023) to debias the headline proportions and differences with the gold subset. Cost: ~25–35 min per document per coder → 100–230 expert-hours; no model calls.
2. **Full-text re-annotation of the reliability sample and of all 381 governance documents** (chunk-and-merge above ~150k tokens; store fraction seen). Compare field by field with the excerpt annotation to quantify truncation bias, especially mechanisms, named instruments, level-2 principles and instrument_status. Cost: ~540 calls, long inputs; ≈ 60–120 USD.
3. **Metadata-blind, header-stripped re-annotation** of the 160-document sample (remove `region_folder`, `collector_title`, `detected_language`, `text_status`, and the crawler header). Measures how much of the high kappa on country, issuer type, language and page quality is leakage. Cost: 160 calls ≈ 7 USD (Sonnet) + 16 USD (Opus).
4. **Prompt-paraphrase and codebook-order sensitivity.** Three semantically equivalent rewrites of the codebook (and one with field order shuffled) on the 160 sample; report per-field kappa range and which labels move (expected: stance, framing, principle level 2, `none`). Cost: ~640 calls ≈ 28 USD.
5. **Cross-vendor second annotator** (a non-Anthropic frontier model) on the 160 sample under the identical prompt and schema, to separate shared-family bias from task ambiguity. Cost: ≈ 10–30 USD.
6. **Lexicon repair and precision audit.** Fix the regexes in M1, hand-check 30 random hits per lexicon (~2,000 snippets, 5–8 hours), report precision, recompute Table 18, Figure 36 and the Spearman correlations on substantive captures only. Cost: analyst time only.
7. **Positional ablation on 100 long documents:** head-only vs head+middle+tail vs full text, to identify which fields depend on omitted parts and whether the model "knows" the content (fame bias) by comparing famous vs obscure instruments. Cost: ~300 calls ≈ 15–40 USD.
8. **Repeat sampling** (5 runs per document on 100 documents) to estimate per-field self-consistency, build majority-vote labels, and measure how many headline labels are coin-flips. Cost: 500 calls ≈ 22 USD.
9. **Deduplication pass and instrument-level recomputation** (title_en + issuer + year clustering, language versions merged), then rerun all counts and tests with one row per instrument or cluster-robust tests. Cost: analyst time only.
10. **Stratified and debiased re-analysis:** all embodied-vs-general and period comparisons within doc_class; principles at level 2 for regional tests; exclusion of inferred dates and 2026; bootstrap CIs on Cramér's V and on the governance-gap index. Cost: analyst time only.
11. **Confidence calibration** against the gold standard from (1): reliability diagram, expected calibration error, and per-field confidence elicitation in a revised schema. Cost: 160 calls ≈ 7 USD plus (1).
