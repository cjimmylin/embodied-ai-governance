What we found when we read every rule on Earth for machines that think and move

Somewhere, right now, a robot is being built to live in your mother's room.

Not a factory arm behind a fence. A machine that lifts, carries, reminds, watches, and talks back.

So we went looking for the rules

Laws with teeth: break them and something happens to you. Each counted once, paperwork excluded.

When the intelligence has a body, the questions change

Can it hurt you?

How close may it come?

Who pays when it breaks something?

How much may it decide alone?

Physical safety is spelled out in 167 of the 278 rules. Fairness in 3. Human dignity in 1. The rulebook for a body is about the body.

Three things, before the robots arrive

1

Write the rules first this time.

Companions, carers, humanoids: zero, four, zero. We can see them coming. Drones did not get that courtesy.

2

Answer the control question. Do not just raise it.

Who may let the machine decide what, under which conditions, checked by whom. Aviation did it. Everyone else can.

3

Make the safety knowledge public.

The documents that say how close, how fast, how much force are standards, and 57 of our 61 are behind a paywall. Rules you cannot read are rules you cannot follow.

The question is not whether robots will come into our homes.

It is whether the rules will be there when they do.
