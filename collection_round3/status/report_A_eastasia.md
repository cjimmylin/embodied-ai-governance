## Things worth knowing

- **Hosts and how they failed.** `flk.npc.gov.cn` (403), `law.go.kr` (empty/reset), `sso.agc.gov.sg` (403), `caat.or.th` (403), `law.moea.gov.tw` (403), `tj.gov.cn` (403) refuse the crawler outright. `elegislation.gov.hk` redirects non-browser clients to a browser-capability check page (`checkClientConfig.jsp`), which was not worked around. `law.moj.gov.tw` loads, but (a) Python 3.13's strict X.509 checks reject its chain ("Missing Subject Key Identifier") and (b) the whole law text sits inside an ASP.NET `<form>`, which `r3fetch.html_to_text` strips, so even headless Chromium yields 0 words; its `/api/Ch/Law/JSON` endpoint returns a 6 MB zip of all laws, unusable here. Many mainland hosts (gz.gov.cn, gzrd, rd.gd.gov.cn, tj.gov.cn, hangzhou.gov.cn, zjrd, zj.gov.cn, suzhou.gov.cn, ahrd https, ordos, nmgrd, sxrd, zhuhai, huzhou, jiaxing, csrd, whrd, szrd) reset the connection; the agent-proxy status log attributes several of these to the egress tunnel closing ("ws_closed_mid_exchange"), and jsrd.gov.cn / hzrd.gov.cn / caa.gov.tw were reachable one minute and reset the next, so 'blocked' for those means unreachable from this environment rather than a documented crawler ban. `sz.gov.cn` fails OpenSSL 3 ("bad ecpoint") but loads in headless Chromium, which is how the three Shenzhen regulations were taken. `www.npc.gov.cn` only speaks a cipher suite OpenSSL/Chromium here cannot negotiate; `legco.gov.hk` and `caa.lk` omit their intermediate certificate (Hongkong Post e-Cert SSL CA 3-17, Sectigo DV R36): I downloaded those intermediates from the CAs' own repositories (www1.ecert.gov.hk, crt.sectigo.com), appended them to a copy of the proxy CA bundle and ran r3fetch with `REQUESTS_CA_BUNDLE` pointing at it — verification stayed on; this is how HK (3) and Sri Lanka were recovered.
- **Two small changes to `scripts/r3fetch.py`** (backup of the original at `/tmp/claude-0/-home-user-embodied-ai-governance/92a26033-b014-57db-85f3-dc52d2f52803/scratchpad/r3fetch.py.orig`, both marked `round-3 (agent A)` in the source): (1) the word count now also counts CJK characters (1 word ≈ 2 characters), because Chinese/Japanese/Korean pages have no spaces and whole regulations were being rejected as 'too short' (e.g. the Beijing drone provisions scored 41 words); (2) when the element picked by the id regex is nearly empty (<200 characters — e.g. an empty `<a id="ariaTipText">` on sz.gov.cn pages matched `text`), the extractor falls back to `<body>`. Neither change alters identification or access behaviour, but the body fallback can let a navigation-only page pass the floor: it did so once (AS-018, Hubei approval resolution page) and I deleted that file. Other collectors' later runs will use the patched script.
