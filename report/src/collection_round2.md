## Summary

The first collection contained 1,656 documents, of which 65 were texts of instrument class in the embodied-AI set and 34 were robot-specific. Round 2 searched official legal databases, government repositories, standards catalogues, treaty-body archives and regulator sites for instruments that the first collection had not captured. After de-duplication against the first collection and within the round, 2,075 candidates remained (1,157 from nine specialised collector agents and 918 from eight scripted harvester runs over official legal databases). Text could be obtained for 1,720 of them (1,704 fetched from the issuing source and 16 standards scope texts; four of these were recovered in a retry pass after the review of the previous version), 1,720 were annotated with the same metadata-blind, extended-excerpt policy as the first collection's governance documents (up to 110,000 characters), and 1,641 entered the master table after 79 duplicates were removed (identical normalised title and jurisdiction, mostly against other round-2 rows).

The blind annotation confirms that most additions are legal texts rather than commentary, and the counting unit then matters. Of the 1,626 usable round-2 documents, 1,351 are texts of instrument class and 988 are principal instruments; 745 of the instrument-class texts are in the embodied-AI set, of which 444 are principal instruments and 378 are principal instruments rated robot-relevant (relevance 2 or 3). The 301 embodied-set texts that are not principal instruments are 218 catalogue or register pages, 35 metadata stubs and 48 ancillary, dependent, incorporation or language-copy acts. Among binding texts, 286 embodied-set texts resolve into 190 principal binding instruments, 164 of them robot-relevant, 135 with autonomy or AI provisions in the text and 10 naming AI explicitly: most binding rules that reach robots in this collection are machine-class rules (aviation, road vehicle, machinery, product safety) with no AI content.

Across the merged collection, on the comparable unit (every text of instrument class), embodied-AI governance instruments rose from 65 to 810 (x12.5), robot-specific instruments from 34 to 699, and binding embodied instruments from 5 to 291. These ratios describe where the two collections searched, not a change in the world: the first collection searched the web for material about embodied AI; the second searched legal databases for the machine-class rules. On the headline unit the merged collection holds 505 principal embodied instruments (408 robot-relevant; 395 with autonomy or AI provisions and 153 naming AI explicitly) and 195 principal binding instruments (168 robot-relevant; 194 when counted once per instrument family; 140 with autonomy or AI provisions and 15 naming AI explicitly; T53b). The coverage audit against the 39 reference instruments moved from 11 captured in the first collection to 22 on the strict criterion (a principal instrument with a content-analysed capture) and from 12 to 31 on the looser title-family match used previously. Still missing under the strict criterion: Germany Autonomous Driving Act (StVG amendment, Level 4) 2021; Korea Intelligent Robots Development and Distribution Promotion Act (2008); EU revised Product Liability Directive (EU) 2024/2853; China Shenzhen Intelligent and Connected Vehicles regulation (2022); China Beijing humanoid robot / embodied-intelligence measures (2024-2025); ISO/TS 15066:2016 collaborative robots; IEC 61508 functional safety; ISO 26262 road vehicle functional safety; ISO 21448 (SOTIF); ISO/IEC 42001 AI management system; IEEE 7001 transparency of autonomous systems; ANSI/RIA R15.06 industrial robot safety; ANSI/A3 R15.08 industrial mobile robots; Korea AI Basic Act (2024/2025, applies Jan 2026); OECD AI Principles (2019, rev. 2024); Taiwan Industrial Robot Hazard Prevention Standards (1987, amended 2018); UN Regulation No. 171 (DCAS).

## What the blind annotation made of the additions

The excerpt cleaner used for the previous version deleted most of the text of Chinese, Japanese and Korean documents before the model saw it (a rule for Latin-script navigation menus dropped every line without a digit, capital letter or ASCII sentence punctuation). The cleaner was made character-aware and 376 round-2 and 46 round-1 documents in those languages were re-annotated; of these 422 documents, 57 changed embodied-set membership and the number rated as not about AI fell from 205 to 160. Four rules then correct status and class labels the annotation got wrong at scale (standards in development are drafts, mandatory GB standards are binding, CCW working papers and chair's texts are position documents, proposals whose adopted act is present are superseded), and 157 document-level corrections on 91 documents (156 rows concern round-2 documents) were converted from the governance expert's audit of the previous version; in that audit's released row table, 119 of 165 risk-stratified rows carry an error code and 107 a material one, so these are upper bounds for the tables as a whole. ## Do the collectors' catalogue labels agree with the blind reading of the text?

This is a consistency check, not a validation. The collector agents were AI agents of the same model family as the annotator, the 918 harvested rows carry one constant label per source written into the harvester, and no person recorded any of the reference values. On the usable round-2 documents the annotation agrees with the collector label on whether an instrument is legally binding in 93.0% of cases (kappa 0.85, n = 1,608), on jurisdiction in 98.1% (n = 1,624), on year within one in 93.8% (n = 1,542) and on document class in 76.7% (kappa 0.66, n = 1,608). Split by origin, binding-nature agreement is 91.9% (kappa 0.79, n = 754) for collector-agent rows and 93.9% (kappa 0.88, n = 854) for harvested rows. The reliability evidence for round-2 labels is a stratified sample of 160 round-2 documents re-annotated by a second model and by a same-model re-run: median kappa over single-label and ordinal fields 0.86 and 0.91; the weakest fields against the second model are page quality 0.53; stance toward regulation 0.65; publication stage 0.66; date basis 0.70; instrument status 0.75 (tables S09 and S09b).

## Method notes and limitations

- Discovery combined nine collector agents (regional, thematic and standards briefs with explicit exclusion lists of the first collection's holdings) and scripted harvests of EUR-Lex (CELLAR SPARQL, by title and by EuroVoc concept), legislation.gov.uk, the Japanese e-Gov law API, the Chinese State Council policy library, the Australian Federal Register of Legislation, the US eCFR and the Brazilian Camara API (eight harvester runs). Title-searched harvests were filtered with an inclusion/exclusion keyword list before fetching; full-text harvests were not. The collector agents were Claude subagents running in one Claude Code session; the harvesters' instrument type, binding nature and status fields are constants per source.
- Candidates were de-duplicated on normalised URL and on normalised title plus jurisdiction, within the round and against the first collection's registry; the assembly step applies a second title-based pass across the merged collection (79 round-2 rows removed). Byte-identical texts of instrument class count once, and titles that differ only in year, act number, version or amendment wording within one jurisdiction form an instrument family, offered as a further counting unit.
- Counting unit: headline counts use principal instruments; T53b gives the merged collection under each unit and under the two text-based AI-content tiers (autonomy or AI provisions; AI named explicitly). Comparisons with the first collection (T53 to T55) stay on the comparable unit, every text of instrument class, and are consequences of collection design rather than findings about the world; the same holds for the number of jurisdictions with an embodied instrument and for the share of instruments that are binding.
- Capture tier: statistics about what instruments specify in depth use content-analysed captures with at least 150 words; catalogue and landing pages are a separate unit and never enter depth statistics. For ISO and other standards only the public scope or abstract page is available (200 of the 260 round-2 standards are catalogue or landing pages), so those documents are annotated on a short text, flagged as such in the registry, and reported as a separate measurement.
- Texts were fetched from the primary URL with a secondary fallback (CELLAR for EUR-Lex, the UN document API for UNODA and undocs links, the ISO committee mirror for ISO catalogue pages, XML for legislation.gov.uk), and every failed fetch was retried once more after the review of the previous version (four texts recovered). The collectors' shared web-search budget was exhausted part-way through several briefs; later verification relied on direct fetches of official sites. Jurisdictions whose law is not exposed through a reachable database (for example Légifrance, the Singapore statutes site, the Korean, Taiwanese and Hong Kong law portals, the National People's Congress database and several ministry sites) are under-represented, and 189 verified candidates recorded as legally binding remain unread.
- Annotation used the metadata-blind extended-excerpt policy (collector metadata stripped, first 110,000 characters in three slices), so round-2 and first-collection governance documents are directly comparable. The character-aware cleaner (policy tag cjk_clean=v4) replaced the rule that deleted Chinese, Japanese and Korean text, and 422 documents were re-annotated. Four status and class rules and 157 documented document-level corrections from the governance expert's audit are applied after annotation; the statement in the previous version that no manual overrides touched round-2 rows no longer holds (156 of the 157 correction rows concern round-2 documents). Statutes harvested as consolidated texts carry the year the annotation found, which may be the enactment or the consolidation year; time analyses are restricted to 2014 to 2026 with 2026 partial.
- Validity: every label is model-produced. The collector-agreement tables are a consistency check between AI-agent labels, harvester constants and the annotation; the round-2 reliability sample (n = 160; median kappa 0.86 against a second model, 0.91 for a re-run) measures stability between models, not correctness. No person read or labelled any document, and no human validation exists. The precision audit shows that about 54% of the previous version's binding embodied instruments were distinct, binding and substantive, so every instrument count here is an upper bound on precision; the unread candidates make every count a lower bound on coverage within this collection.
- Not addressed: human validation of labels (no human coders available in this setting); full-text annotation of statutes longer than the 110,000-character excerpt, whose robot provisions may lie beyond it; stricter merging of near-duplicate title pairs beyond the instrument-family grouping.
